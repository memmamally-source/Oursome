## Exam structure (as reported)
- One-day exam, up to 280 single-best-answer multiple-choice items, about 8 hours of seat time.
- From 14 May 2026, new test-delivery software: **14 blocks of up to 20 items, 30 minutes each** (previously 7 blocks × 40 items × 60 minutes). Per-item pace is unchanged at about 90 s. Content outline, item formats and scoring were reported as unchanged. Break and tutorial time changes were reported inconsistently by secondary sources: check the official bulletin.
- Scored pass/fail (since January 2022).
- Items test application of foundational science, often within a clinical vignette, plus some items on communication, ethics and patient safety.

## Organ systems
The taxonomy mirrors the outline's structure: general principles; blood/lymphoreticular and immune; behavioural health and nervous system/special senses; musculoskeletal, skin and subcutaneous; cardiovascular; respiratory; renal/urinary; gastrointestinal; reproductive; endocrine; multisystem processes; biostatistics, epidemiology and population health; social sciences (communication, ethics, patient safety). System percentage ranges are left `null` until verified.

## Implications for content
- Prioritise pathology and physiology mechanisms; Step 1 rewards explaining *why* findings occur.
- Every concept is tagged to a primary discipline and system, with additional tags allowed (`alsoDisciplines`, `alsoSystems`).
- Coverage targets (later): weight new-content priorities by blueprint range once verified.

## Resource landscape (desk summary, no protected material used)
Commonly used Step 1 resources include question banks (UWorld, AMBOSS, Kaplan, USMLE-Rx, Bootcamp), review books and video series (First Aid, Pathoma, Boards & Beyond, Sketchy micro/pharm, Lecturio, Osmosis), spaced repetition (Anki with the AnKing deck), biostatistics resources (e.g. Randy Neil) and NBME self-assessments and the official free sample items. We use this landscape only to understand what students study and how (mechanism-first teaching, visual mnemonics, high-volume spaced recall, timed blocks). **No text, questions, images or mnemonics from these resources are used**, and commercial "high-yield" claims are not treated as evidence of exam content.
